Kraft Heinz: Altman's false alarm

Kraft Heinz has been in the Altman Z-Score's distress zone in every 10-K since 2016 and never came close to bankruptcy. Faultline kept it in the middle.

Percentile of bankruptcy risk among all 10-Ks or 10-Qs (each point is one filing) filed in the 365 days up to that filing (never later ones). Shaded bands: top 5% (high) and top 20% (elevated). Each point is scored by a model that had only seen outcomes known before that year.

What the scores said

  • Altman Z-Score: distress zone (below 1.8) in every 10-K from 2017 to 2026, for example 1.41 in 2017, 0.32 in 2019 and 0.96 in 2024. Kraft Heinz never came near bankruptcy.
  • Faultline: after 2017 mostly in the middle of the pack: riskier than 18% of filers in 2018, 43% in 2021 and 14% in 2025.
  • Accounting: in May 2019 Kraft Heinz told investors that earlier financial statements could no longer be relied on (an 8-K Item 4.02 restatement notice). Faultline's restatement score had it riskier than only 3% of filers in its February 2018 10-K: a miss.

Why Altman flags it

The Z-Score was built in 1968 on manufacturers. Kraft Heinz was formed by a 2015 merger, so most of its $90-100 billion of assets is goodwill and brand value. That makes sales look tiny next to assets (about 25-30 cents of sales per dollar of assets), and the merger reset retained earnings to near zero. Both push the Z-Score down even though the company earns billions and pays its bills.

The lesson

A score that flags everyone big and acquisitive isn't useful. Testing against real outcomes is how you find out: in Faultline's test, the Altman Z-Score's riskiest 10% caught 13% of bankruptcies; Faultline's caught 52%.
