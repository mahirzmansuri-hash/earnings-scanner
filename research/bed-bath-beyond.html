Bed Bath & Beyond: Altman said safe

The Altman Z-Score put Bed Bath & Beyond in its safe zone every year until a year before it went bankrupt. Faultline's score climbed for six straight years.

Percentile of bankruptcy risk among all 10-Ks or 10-Qs (each point is one filing) filed in the 365 days up to that filing (never later ones). Shaded bands: top 5% (high) and top 20% (elevated). Each point is scored by a model that had only seen outcomes known before that year.

What the scores said

  • Faultline climbed every year: riskier than 30% of filers in April 2016, 63% in 2019, 95% in April 2021 (two years before the bankruptcy) and 99% in April 2022. Its 10-Qs were in the riskiest 5% from September 2021 on.
  • Altman Z-Score said safe (above 3) in every 10-K through April 2022: 6.69 in 2016 and still 4.32 in April 2022, a year before the company filed for bankruptcy on April 23, 2023.

Why Altman missed it

The Z-Score adds up five ratios. In the April 2022 10-K, one of them did most of the work: retained earnings were 188% of total assets. Bed Bath had decades of past profits on its books, and spending billions on buying back its own shares didn't reduce that number. Meanwhile, liabilities had reached 97% of assets ($4.96 billion of $5.13 billion), operating cash flow had fallen from about $1 billion a year to $18 million, and sales had dropped by a third since 2019.

Faultline looks at those things directly: debt load, cash flow, falling sales, and their trend. That's why its score rose while the classic score stayed calm.

The lesson

A single famous formula can be fooled by one odd number. Retained earnings measure the past; bankruptcy is about the next two years.
